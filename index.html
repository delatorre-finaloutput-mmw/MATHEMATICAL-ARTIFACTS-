Object 01 of 05

Clock and Watches

Mesopotamia / Babylon
A clock or watch used to measure time.
It is connected to Ancient Mesopotamia (Babylonia) through the development of the sexagesimal (base-60) number system, found in Babylonian mathematical tablets.
This system was used for astronomy, time measurement, and calculations involving circles and celestial movement.
Today, it remains the basis of modern time (60 seconds, 60 minutes).
Object 02 of 05

Set Square and Triangle

Egypt
A set square used for constructing and measuring right angles and triangles.
It is connected to Ancient Egypt, especially through the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus, which shows practical geometry used in surveying and construction.
Egyptians used geometry to re-establish land boundaries after Nile floods and design large structures like pyramids.
Today, it is essential in architecture, engineering, and technical drawing.
Object 03 of 05

Mobile Phone Compass

China
A mobile phone compass used for navigation and direction.
It is connected to Ancient China, where early magnetic direction tools such as the lodestone compass (south-pointing device) were developed.
This was used for navigation, orientation, and travel on land and sea.
Today, smartphones use digital sensors based on magnetism for GPS and navigation systems.
Object 04 of 05

Scientific Calculator

Mesopotamia / Babylon
A scientific calculator used for advanced mathematical computations.
It is connected to Ancient Mesopotamia (Sumerians and Babylonians) through Sumerian multiplication tables and Babylonian clay tablets, which show early structured arithmetic systems and problem-solving methods.
These were used for trade, taxation, construction, and astronomy calculations.
Today, calculators apply the same arithmetic principles for education, science, and engineering.
Object 05 of 05

Calendar

Maya
A calendar used to organize time and dates.
It is connected to the Maya Civilization, which developed highly accurate astronomical calendar systems (Tzolk'in and Haab' cycles) based on mathematical observation of celestial movements.
These were used for agriculture, rituals, and predicting seasonal cycles.
Today, calendars remain essential for scheduling, time management, and global coordination.
